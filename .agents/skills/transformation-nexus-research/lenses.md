# Seven Analytical Lenses for Transformation Research

### 1. George Westerman (MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy)
- **Core Published Concept:** Digital Mastery requires two independent dimensions: *Digital Capability* (the "what": customer experience, operational processes, business models) and *Leadership Capability* (the "how": governance, digital vision, employee engagement).
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Fashionistas buy tools without leadership; Conservatives have governance without investment; Masters align both.
- **Key Reference:** Westerman, Bonnet, & McAfee (2014), *Leading Digital: Turning Technology into Business Transformation*.

### 2. Andrew McAfee (MIT Sloan)
- **Core Published Concept:** Shift from HiPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion) to evidence-based decision making; unbundling traditional hierarchies in favor of platforms, decentralized talent, and telemetry.
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Does the system provide frontline workers with actionable data, or does it simply generate retrospective executive reporting?
- **Key Reference:** McAfee & Brynjolfsson (2017), *Machine, Platform, Crowd: Harnessing Our Digital Future*.

### 3. Erik Brynjolfsson (Stanford Digital Economy Lab)
- **Core Published Concept:** The Productivity J-Curve. General Purpose Technologies (GPTs) like ERP, cloud, and AI show an initial productivity lull or decline because intangible investments (retraining, business process redesign, data cleaning) are not capitalized as current output. Compounding gains occur only after complements are mature.
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Did the organization plan for the J-curve dip, or did short-term panic lead to prematurely cutting transformation investments?
- **Key Reference:** Brynjolfsson, Rock, & Syverson (2021), *The Productivity J-Curve: How Intangibles Complement General Purpose Technologies*.

### 4. Michael Hammer (Reengineering)
- **Core Published Concept:** "Don't Automate, Obliterate." Automating an inefficient legacy process merely speeds up waste. Transformation requires questioning underlying assumptions and removing handoffs, reconciliations, and checks.
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Which legacy reconciliations, batch intervals, or verification checkpoints were eliminated permanently?
- **Key Reference:** Hammer & Champy (1993), *Reengineering the Corporation: A Manifesto for Business Revolution*.

### 5. Nicholas Carr
- **Core Published Concept:** "IT Doesn't Matter" (as a proprietary differentiator). When technology becomes ubiquitous, standardized, and accessible to all competitors, it shifts from an offensive weapon to defensive table-stakes infrastructure. Advantage resides in risk management, cost efficiency, and proprietary execution discipline.
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Is the enterprise treating commodity ERP capabilities as a secret weapon, or focusing true differentiation on proprietary plant algorithms and operational rigor?
- **Key Reference:** Carr (2004), *Does IT Matter? Information Technology and the Corrosion of Competitive Advantage*.

### 6. Daniel Schallmo
- **Core Published Concept:** Digital Transformation of Business Models. Systematic mapping across the four pillars: Value Proposition (what is offered), Customer Segments & Channels (who receives it), Value Chain / Network (how it is created), and Revenue Model (how it captures value).
- **Critical Diagnostic:** Did the transformation change only internal production costs, or did it transform the external customer value proposition?
- **Key Reference:** Schallmo & Williams (2018), *Digital Transformation Now! Guiding the Successful Digitalization of Your Business Model*.

### 7. Ming Zeng (Alibaba Group / Smart Business)
- **Core Published Concept:** Smart Business. The convergence of Network Coordination (coordinating activities via internet protocols without hierarchical friction) and Data Intelligence (applying machine learning feedback loops to live operations).
- **Critical Diagnostic:** How quickly does customer and operational telemetry loop back into operational decisions?
- **Key Reference:** Zeng (2018), *Smart Business: What Alibaba's Success Reveals about the Future of Strategy*.
